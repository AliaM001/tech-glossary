Curriculum review & sources

The original 25 terms provided a useful design-process foundation with examples in five domains. This review expands the glossary to 220 entries: 59 shared terms and 161 specialist entries. Every shared term has junior and senior examples across the six core technology domains; 16 also have applications in ECE, Baking and Hospitality. Each new subject has 24 specialist entries, with examples at both explanation levels.

Systems and Control is now a separate domain. Resistant materials includes the original hard-materials context; Spatial and Product Design includes the original design and visual communication context. Digital Technology connects to Year 11 Digital Technologies, Computer Science and Digital Media.

Early Childhood Education (ECE), Baking and Hospitality: ECE vocabulary draws on Te Whāriki; Baking and Hospitality use subject, production, service and food-safety references. These references are labelled separately from Technology curriculum connections. Junior and senior refer to the secondary learner’s explanation level, including for ECE studies, rather than the age of children in an early childhood setting.

What does “sufficient vocabulary” mean here?

This is a broad foundation for staff planning, assessed by coverage of curriculum strands and recurring disciplinary concepts, rather than a minimum word count. It supports vocabulary teaching across NZC 2007+, Phase 4 and the proposed Phase 5 subject pathways. It is not a statement-by-statement certification of a teaching programme or an NCEA assessment guide.

Specialist entries group some related concepts. Staff should unpack them in their units, teach the exact tools, techniques, ingredients, materials and software used, and revisit vocabulary as the proposed senior content develops. Junior explanations make advanced terms approachable; they do not imply that all senior concepts are compulsory in Years 9–10.

Curriculum status and mapping
  • NZC 2007+: the existing Technology framework, including the 2017 digital technology revision. Review connections cover technological practice, technological knowledge and nature of technology, including all eight achievement-objective components.
  • Phase 4: Years 9–10 content in the 2026 National Curriculum Statement. The review uses Schedule 2, pp. 274–280: Design, Make, and Innovate; Spatial and Product Design; Materials Technology; Food and Processing; Systems and Control; and Digital Technology.
  • Phase 5: the eight October 2026 subject teaching documents. Tāhūrangi identifies this content as proposed, with formal status and assessment information pending and staged use planned from 2028–2030. The current senior curriculum remains in effect during transition.

Each card links to the relevant source and location. “Teaching connection” is an editorial mapping, not a claim that the exact glossary term appears verbatim in a mandatory statement. Level and subject buttons change the examples shown. The reference filter shows entries with a mapped curriculum connection or subject reference; it is not a year-level teaching checklist.

Changes to the original glossary

All original entries and examples are retained. Systems and Control examples are added to every original entry. Seven definitions are revised: Attribute, Context, Critical evaluation, Manipulating materials, Sustainability, Transformation processes, and Usability testing. The revisions avoid rigid distinctions, acknowledge contextual judgement and remove an assumption that usability tests must use think-aloud.

Numerical values in examples are illustrative design targets or scenarios. Use appropriate expert guidance and school procedures when establishing actual safety limits or operating requirements.
