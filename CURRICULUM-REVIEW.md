# Technology glossary curriculum review

Reviewed 11 November 2026. Definitions, new examples and curriculum connections are editorial teaching explanations, not Ministry wording or an official endorsement.

## Finding

The original 25 entries provided a useful design-process foundation, each with junior and senior examples in five domains. They were **not broad enough as the sole vocabulary reference** against all three curriculum contexts requested. Systems, technological knowledge, responsible practice and disciplinary vocabulary needed expansion.

The revised edition has **148 entries: 59 shared and 89 specialist**. Every shared entry has junior and senior examples in all six domains. Specialist examples use authentic disciplinary applications. Systems and Control is added as a separate domain. Updated labels retain the original hard-materials and design/visual-communication contexts.

This is a broad staff vocabulary foundation. Sufficiency depends on the programme and context: a glossary cannot establish that all required knowledge and practices have been taught. Grouped entries introduce several concepts that teachers should unpack explicitly. Junior explanations introduce advanced vocabulary; they do not imply that all senior concepts are required in Years 9–10.

## Definition review

All original entries and their five-domain examples are retained. Systems examples are added. Seven definitions are revised:

- Attribute: avoids claiming that attributes must always be qualitative.
- Context: includes social, cultural, historical and economic circumstances.
- Critical evaluation: includes processes, impacts and evidence beyond a brief.
- Manipulating materials: avoids claiming that manipulation cannot change any properties.
- Sustainability: recognises evidence and trade-offs rather than promising no harm.
- Transformation processes: includes composition and biological/chemical changes.
- Usability testing: makes think-aloud an optional method.

Numerical examples are illustrative design scenarios, not validated safety limits. Use appropriate school procedures, current product information and relevant expertise for actual operating or safety requirements.

## Vocabulary still specific to each programme

Complete unit planning also needs the exact tools, techniques, ingredients, materials, software and standards used. This glossary does not catalogue every operation or command. Extend it with:

- Food: specific ingredient chemistry, validated safety procedures and product-specific tests.
- Textiles: particular pattern adjustments, seams/finishes, fibre tests, measurement protocols and appropriate cultural practices.
- Resistant materials: machinery/tools, material subtypes, joints, workshop conventions and actual tolerances.
- Spatial/product design: chosen precedents, local cultural context, site vocabulary and detailed drawing conventions.
- Digital: selected algorithms, SQL commands, language constructs, network protocols, AI methods and media-production techniques.
- Systems: component ratings, circuit calculations, selected protocols, interface circuits, control methods and measurement procedures.

The term coverage CSV shows domains, levels and references. Revisit mappings when curriculum versions change. No claim is made that this review checks every teaching statement or NCEA requirement.
